A family-run restaurant on Kindaruma Road, where the cooking is African, the grill runs all day, and the best tables are outside.

01 — Kilimani

Karibu.

Welcome — come in

Set back from Kindaruma Road.

Château Rouge is a house restaurant in Kilimani: a gate, parking, a garden, and a dining room lined with African art. It is family-run, and it feels like it — the same faces at the door, the same welcome, the table you had last time if it is free.

Most guests head outside. The garden is green and unhurried, with tables under the trees and the kitchen a few steps away, which is why the food arrives hot and the evening takes its time.

02 — The kitchen

Moto.

Fire

Fish, chicken, fire.

The kitchen is not trying to reinvent Kenyan food. It buys fish and chicken, cooks them hard and fast, and sends them out with the sides people actually want. Whole fish fried until the skin crackles. Chicken chopped and dry-fried with onion and chilli. Samosas out of the fryer, hot enough to warn you.

The menu is longer than the six dishes on the board, and it moves with the day. That is why the site tells you to call rather than promising you a printed card: what is good on Tuesday is not always what is good on Sunday.

03 — The room

Meza kubwa.

The long table

Built for a table of twelve.

Birthdays, family lunches, weddings, meetings that run past lunch — the room is used to groups, and the garden handles them better than a dining room with walls. Ask for the long table when you book, and tell them how many are coming.

Service is unhurried and the room is not quiet. If you are used to being rushed, adjust. If you came to sit for three hours and eat a whole fish, you are in the right place.

  • African cooking Fish, chicken, small plates, cooked plainly.
  • Garden tables Under the trees; ask when you book.
  • Parking on site Off Kindaruma Road.
  • Open daily, 10am – midnight Breakfast through dinner.
